# Metabolomics Signatures of Cardio-Kidney-Metabolic Syndrome in Younger-Onset Type 2 Diabetes
Project duration: 2026.8.20 ~ 2026.10.7
Present during the 2026 Singapore Health & Biomedical Conference SHBC (Oct.8 & 9)

This is one of my project done during my internship at KTPH, with the guidance and support by my supervisor Mrs.Clara and research officer Kai Xiang. The research data was provided by clinical research unit, KTPH.

**Background**
Younger-onset type 2 diabetes (YT2D), defined as onset age <40, confers disproportionate cardiovascular risk and is a key driver of cardio-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome. The metabolomic landscape underlying CKM-related atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) and heart failure (HF) risk in YT2D remains poorly characterised. We aimed to identify plasma metabolites associated with 10-year ASCVD and HF risk in a multi-ethnic YT2D cohort as proxies for the cardiovascular burden of CKM syndrome.

**Methods**
AHA PREVENT-derived 10-year ASCVD and HF risk scores were calculated for 235 and 263 YT2D patients respectively. 249 plasma metabolites were measured using NMR spectroscopy (Nightingale Health). Metabolome-wide associations were assessed by multivariable linear regression, adjusting for age, sex, diabetes duration, HbA1c, and eGFR, with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction.

**Results**
Median age was 36 years and diabetes duration was 8 years. 74 metabolites were significantly associated with ASCVD risk (FDR<0.05), dominated by triglyceride-rich lipoprotein subfractions, monounsaturated fatty acids, saturated fatty acids, and GlycA, reflecting a broad dyslipidaemic and pro-inflammatory milieu. In contrast, only three metabolites were significantly associated with HF risk: plasma creatinine (β=0.56), IDL cholesteryl ester proportion (β=-0.16), and XS-VLDL phospholipid proportion (β=0.15), pointing to renal-lipoprotein crosstalk as a distinct HF-related pathway. Plasma creatinine was the strongest signal across both outcomes, consistent with the renal axis of CKM syndrome.

**Conclusion**
The metabolomic architecture of CKM-related cardiovascular risk in YT2D is outcome-specific. The broad dyslipidaemic signature of ASCVD contrasts with the parsimonious renal-lipoprotein profile of HF, highlighting distinct metabolic pathways that may inform targeted prevention strategies across the CKM spectrum.
